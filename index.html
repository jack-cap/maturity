Why timing matters

A good rate is only part of the decision

Put one large amount into one long deposit and the months in between can show no returning principal at all. That may earn interest, but it also concentrates your liquidity into a single date.

One large maturity

Nothing is scheduled to return in the months between placement and maturity. The whole portfolio needs a new decision on the same date.

Maturities across time

Each month becomes visible

Returning principal is spread across the calendar. You can see the amount due back in each period and decide what to place next.

The aim is not to leave more money idle. It is to know when money is needed, then keep shorter-term placements visible while you wait.
How Maturity helps

Plan from the portfolio you actually hold

Maturity brings today’s available cash and the next twelve months of recorded maturities into one view. It gives you the schedule; every placement decision remains yours.

  1. 1

    Start with today

    Available cash is the exact ledger total for the selected currency. Open it to see which account and bank can actually fund a deposit now.

  2. 2

    Read the months ahead

    The maturity ladder shows principal and interest due back by month or quarter. Empty periods stay visible, because the gaps explain the shape of the portfolio.

  3. 3

    Record the next move

    Add the deposit you choose. If money is waiting for a later month, a recorded shorter-term deposit remains in the schedule until it becomes available again.

Your strategy, clearly recorded

Built for real records

The view stays useful when reality changes

Deposits roll, cash moves between banks, quoted interest differs and currencies cannot honestly be added together. Maturity keeps those details explicit instead of smoothing them away.

⇆

Cash you can reconcile

Cash is tracked by account and bank. Transfers record what left and what arrived separately, and reconciliation records the difference from the balance your bank shows.

  • Running balance on every ledger row
  • Account-level available cash
  • Dated corrections instead of silent edits

Calculations you can inspect

The interest maths, stated plainly

Products use different day-count bases. Maturity uses actual/365 by default, while actual/360 and 30/360 are supported per deposit. Every calculated figure is a starting point you can replace with your bank’s quote.

Money is stored as whole cents and rates as whole basis points. Your balance is the exact sum of your transactions, not a floating-point approximation.

Interest on USD 100,000 at 4.85% p.a.
TermBasisInterest
365 days Actual/365 $4,850.00
90 days Actual/365 $1,195.89
90 days Actual/360 $1,212.50

The basis changes the result. Select the convention quoted for each product, then replace the estimate if your institution calculates it differently.

Private by design

Your portfolio is not the product

Maturity has no developer-operated server, no account to create and no analytics. Your records remain under your control.

Stored on your devices

Records live on your iPhone, iPad and Mac and, when available, sync through your own private iCloud container using CloudKit.

No banking credentials

Maturity does not connect to financial institutions. You enter each record yourself, so there is no bank login to hand to the app.

No developer analytics

No analytics SDKs, advertising identifiers or third-party crash reporting. If you enable indicative conversion, the reference-rate provider receives requested currency codes and ordinary connection metadata, never portfolio amounts or record identifiers.
