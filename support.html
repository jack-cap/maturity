Support

Maturity for iPhone, iPad and Mac.

Get in touch: open an issue at github.com/jack-cap/maturity/issues. Bug reports, calculation queries and feature requests all welcome. Please say which device and OS version you're on, and for a calculation question include the deposit terms so the figures can be reproduced.

Getting started

  1. Set your opening balance. Enter the cash you can actually deploy right now — the balance showing in the account you place deposits from. Don't add the principal of deposits you already hold.
  2. Add the deposits you already hold. Enter each with its real start date, even if that's months ago. On each one set Funding to “already open before I started tracking”, and no cash movement is recorded for the principal — your bank took that money out before this ledger existed.
  3. Check the figure. Free cash should already match your bank. If it doesn't, use Reconcile cash to record the difference.

The one thing worth getting right. Free cash is the sum of your transaction history. If you enter today's bank balance as your opening figure and then let a deposit you opened last week subtract its principal, that money comes out twice — once by your bank, once by the app — and you'll be short by the principal. The Funding setting on each deposit is there to prevent exactly that.

Frequently asked

How do I add deposits I already hold?

Enter each one normally, with its real start date, and set Funding to “already open before I started tracking”. That records the deposit without a cash movement, because the principal left your account before your ledger existed. The deposit still appears in Positions, still drives the ladder, and still credits your cash when it matures.

There's a second approach that also works, if you prefer it: set your opening balance to your cash plus the principal of every deposit you hold, then leave each deposit funded from free cash as normal. The arithmetic lands in the same place. It just means remembering the rule for every deposit, and one slip leaves you short.

Why is my free cash lower than my bank shows?

Almost always because a deposit you opened before you started tracking is set to be funded from free cash. The app subtracts the principal, but your bank already subtracted it, so the same money is gone twice.

Open the deposit, change Funding to “already open before I started tracking”, and save. The cash movement is removed and your free cash goes back up by the principal. If the figures still disagree after that, use Reconcile cash to record the remaining difference.

What are cash accounts for?

Cash sitting at one institution can't fund a deposit at another — it has to move first, and that takes days. So “how much can I place today” is only answerable per account. Accounts also make reconciliation match reality: you check one bank's balance at a time, not a combined figure.

If all your cash sits in one place, ignore this entirely. One account is created automatically and the app won't mention accounts anywhere until you add a second.

What do I need to set up before the app is useful?

Four things, and Summary shows them as a checklist until they're done:

1. Your currency. Everything is denominated per currency and totals are never mixed across them, so this decides what new records default to. Pick it straight from the checklist; add more in Settings if you hold deposits in several.

2. Your bank accounts. One per place your cash sits, because cash at one bank can't fund a deposit at another without moving first. Recording the bank on each account is what groups them together everywhere.

3. What each account holds. An opening balance per account, which is what free cash is measured from. You can set one for every account, not just the first.

4. Deposits you already hold. Optional, but if you have term deposits already running, add them with already open before tracking as the funding source so your cash isn't reduced twice for money that left before you started.

On a new install these appear as a guided flow, one stage at a time, and it stays open until you leave it — so it can't vanish while you're halfway through entering the deposits you hold. Afterwards a single-line reminder on Summary names the next outstanding thing and reopens the flow. Dismiss it there, or bring it back from Settings.

Each stage is read from your data rather than a saved flag, so nothing claims to be done when it isn't — the balances stage needs an opening balance for every account you've set up, not just the first — and nothing nags on a second device, where the portfolio arrives already complete. Only the currency and one account are required; deposits are optional, since someone starting fresh has none.

Interest conventions don't need setting up: actual/365, at-maturity interest and 31 days' break notice are the Australian retail defaults and are already in place. Change them in Settings if your product quotes differently. There's nothing to configure for iCloud, and no exchange rates to supply, since the app never combines currencies.

Can I reorder my accounts, or group them by bank?

Both, and the grouping is automatic. Record a bank on each account and Maturity groups accounts by it everywhere they appear — in Settings, in "Where your cash is", and in every account dropdown. Accounts at the same institution are always adjacent, however late you added them, so you never have to scan a long list to find the second account at a bank you already use.

A new account is also slotted directly after its siblings rather than appended to the end, so the stored order agrees with what you see. When adding one, the bank field offers the institutions you've already recorded, which keeps one bank from becoming three spellings.

For manual order, open an account's menu in Settings and choose Move up or Move down to reorder it within its bank, or Move <bank> up / down to move that whole bank. The arrows beside a bank's heading do the same thing. One ordering drives every list, so the sequence you set in Settings is the sequence you get in the dropdowns. Accounts with no bank recorded gather at the end under their own heading.

How do transfers between my accounts work?

You enter both amounts: what left one account and what arrived in the other. They're two separate facts off two separate statements, and it's the arriving figure that tells you what you can actually deploy at the destination. Same-currency transfers default the second amount to match the first, and stay editable if a different figure landed.

Because both sides are recorded as they happened, transfers work across currencies with no exchange rate involved. Enter the AUD that left and the HKD that arrived; each balance stays exact. A transfer never changes how much you hold overall, only where it sits — so it isn't treated as spending and earning. Deleting one side removes both, since a half-transfer would show money vanishing.

Can I track deposits in more than one currency?

Yes. Add the currencies you use in Settings and pick one as your default. Each deposit and each cash movement is denominated in a single currency, chosen when you create it, and every figure is shown with that currency's own symbol.

Free cash, weighted average yield and the maturity ladder are all reported separately per currency. There is deliberately no combined total. Adding two currencies together requires an exchange rate, and a rate would make your headline balance depend on when it was last fetched — so a converted figure would be an estimate wearing the clothes of a balance. Reconciliation is per currency too, which matches how you actually check it: one bank account at a time.

How do I compare currencies if they're never added up?

By comparing the figures that don't carry a currency. Summary opens on an overview listing every currency side by side, and beneath it a comparison of weighted average rate, idle cash as a proportion, and average term remaining in days. All three are percentages or durations, so no exchange rate is involved and nothing goes stale. The app will also tell you the spread — how far the leading currency is ahead, in basis points.

Bars on that chart are always rates, never balances. A bar chart of two balances in different currencies would be two unrelated units drawn against one axis, which is meaningless however convincing it looks.

One caveat is stated on the card itself: a higher rate in one currency is not a better return in another. Moving money across means taking on exchange-rate movement, which can easily exceed the difference in yield, and this app neither tracks nor forecasts rates.

Can I see one total across all my currencies?

Yes, as an estimate, and only if you ask for it. Turn on Show an indicative total in Settings under Currency. Summary then shows one converted figure for free cash, principal on deposit and total position, each marked with ≈ and shown with the date of the rate used.

It is deliberately walled off from everything else. The conversion is never stored, never applied to a balance, and never used in a yield or interest figure — your recorded numbers do not change when a rate does. That was the whole reason the app avoided combined totals to begin with: a stored converted balance would move on its own every time a rate was refetched.

Rates are published daily reference rates from central banks, retrieved from Frankfurter, and they only cover about thirty major currencies. If one of yours isn't covered the app says so and leaves those amounts out of the estimate rather than guessing. A real transfer will differ — your bank's rate and fees aren't in here.

Enabling this is the only thing that makes the app contact anything other than your own iCloud. The request contains currency codes and nothing else; see the privacy policy for exactly what is and isn't sent. Turning it back off deletes the cached rates.

What happens if I set an opening balance twice?

The second one replaces the first rather than adding to it. An account has exactly one opening balance — the point its history starts — so the sheet tells you what's already recorded and the button reads Replace balance.

This matters because free cash is the sum of your whole transaction history rather than a stored number. Two opening balances on one account would silently count the same money twice, with nothing on screen to indicate it. To record money arriving after you started tracking, use Cash in instead — that's a movement, not a starting point.

Can I change how the ladder is grouped?

Yes. The ladder buckets by month or by quarter. Quarters are the default because they're the compact end: a sparse ladder shown as twelve monthly columns is mostly empty space around one bar. Your choice is remembered between launches.

Each period is a single stacked column, with principal and interest in the same bar, so a period's total is its height and the split within it is visible in the same place. Point at any period to see its exact principal, interest and paying institutions.

Why does my currency show as A$ rather than $?

Because your device's region differs from the currency. When the region is, say, Hong Kong and the amount is Australian dollars, the system adds the country prefix so the two dollars aren't confused. Set your device region to match your main currency if you'd prefer the plain symbol.

How is interest calculated?

Simple interest accrued daily: principal × rate × days ÷ 365. That actual/365 basis is what Australian retail term deposits use. Actual/360 and 30/360 are also selectable per deposit for products that quote on a different convention. Interest is rounded to the nearest cent at the end of each accrual period, which is what banks do, so a periodic-paying deposit can total a cent or two away from a single whole-term calculation.

The app's figure doesn't match my bank's. Which is right?

Your bank's. Every calculated figure in Maturity is editable for exactly this reason — look for the option to use your bank's quoted amount. Common causes of a small difference are a different day-count basis, interest starting the day after deposit rather than the same day, or the bank rounding at different points. Enter the real number and the app records that.

How does breaking a deposit early work?

Australian institutions generally don't touch your principal on an early break. They recalculate the interest at a reduced rate, cut more heavily the earlier you break, and most require around 31 days' notice before releasing funds. Maturity records the notice and the settlement as two separate stages so your cash forecast stays accurate across the gap. It estimates the reduced interest using a typical banded schedule, but the reduction isn't standardised, so ask your bank for the exact figure and type it in.

What happens when a deposit rolls over?

Use Roll over rather than collecting and re-placing. It closes the old deposit, opens the new one, and records both legs, so your history shows the money never passed through your everyday account. You can roll part of the proceeds and keep the rest as cash, which is common when you want to take the interest as income and reinvest only the principal. The new rate is left blank on purpose: banks apply whatever rate is current on the roll date, not your old one.

Why does the app ask me to confirm interest payments?

For deposits that pay monthly or quarterly, the app knows when a payment is due but won't write it to your ledger on its own. Putting unconfirmed figures in your history would work against reconciliation and leave you with numbers you never checked. Instead it prompts you on the Summary screen and you accept with one tap, or amend the amount first.

My devices aren't syncing.

Check that both devices are signed in to the same Apple Account and have iCloud Drive turned on, and that Maturity is enabled in your iCloud settings. Sync needs a network connection and can take a moment to settle. Bringing the app to the foreground on both devices usually prompts an exchange. If a device has no iCloud account at all the app still works and keeps data locally.

Is my data backed up?

If iCloud sync is on, your records exist in your private iCloud container as well as on each device, so losing one device doesn't lose the data. That is not a substitute for a considered backup strategy. The developer holds no copy and cannot recover anything on your behalf.

Can it connect to my bank automatically?

No, by design. Maturity never asks for banking credentials and never contacts a financial institution. Everything is entered by hand, which means there's no credential to leak and no third party sitting between you and your bank.

Does deleting a deposit change my cash balance?

Not by default. Deleting a deposit keeps its cash movements in the ledger, so free cash is unaffected — removing a record shouldn't quietly rewrite your cash history. If you're cleaning up something entered by mistake, choose the option to delete the deposit together with its history.

Reporting a calculation problem

Interest maths is the part worth getting right, so these reports are especially welcome. Include:

  • Principal, rate, start date and maturity date
  • Interest payment frequency and the day-count basis you selected
  • The figure the app showed, and the figure your bank gave you
