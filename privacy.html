Privacy policy

Last updated 1 September 2026. Applies to Maturity for iPhone, iPad and Mac.

The short version. Maturity has no developer-operated server, analytics or account system. Your app records stay on your devices and sync only through your private iCloud container, which the developer cannot access. Maturity reminders and your language choice are managed locally on each device. The optional exchange-rate feature sends currency codes but no portfolio records. Exports are created locally only when you ask. Content you voluntarily submit for support is separate and is handled by GitHub and may be public.

1. What data the app holds

Maturity stores data needed to provide the app, including:

  • User-entered institution and account names, optional account or certificate references, notes and transaction memos
  • User-entered deposit amounts, interest rates, dates, payment frequencies and day-count bases
  • User-entered cash transactions, including opening balances, deposits, interest, maturities, early breaks, fees, transfers and reconciliation adjustments
  • The app derives balances, interest estimates, tracked-position summaries, liquidity views, maturity ladders and attention items from those records on the device; those views are not separate synced records
  • Settings that sync with the portfolio, plus device-local preferences and caches used to operate the app
  • Device-local choices for the interface language and maturity reminders, together with pending notification requests managed by the operating system

The app does not ask for your name, email address, date of birth, location, contacts, photos, banking credentials, advertising identifier or device identifier.

If you enable maturity reminders, the app asks the operating system for notification permission and schedules local alerts for active deposits at 9:00 AM seven days before and on the maturity day. Alerts contain the bank and maturity date but no amount. They are generated on the device and do not pass through a developer-operated server or add another network purpose. Disabling reminders removes Maturity's pending requests; the in-app Delete all data action also clears local preferences and managed reminder requests on that device.

Your selected interface language is stored only on that device, applies immediately and does not alter or translate portfolio records. If Show an indicative equivalent is enabled, the app also caches public exchange rates, their publication dates and the service's supported-currency list on that device. Disabling the feature deletes this fetched cache.

2. Where that data lives

Records are stored locally in the app's container. When you are signed into iCloud with iCloud Drive available, Apple's system-managed CloudKit integration automatically syncs records and synced settings through a private CloudKit container associated with your Apple Account. Apple controls sync scheduling, transport and availability; there is no manual upload to, or developer-operated sync service. Device-local preferences and caches remain on that device.

Apple operates that storage under Apple's privacy policy. Maturity's developer has no credentials or mechanism for reading, exporting or recovering its contents. Without iCloud, the app continues to work with local storage only.

3. What is not collected

  • No analytics or telemetry. The App Store app reports no usage events to the developer.
  • No tracking. No advertising identifiers, fingerprinting, cross-app or cross-site tracking, or data-broker sharing.
  • No third-party SDKs in the app. The App Store binary uses Apple's frameworks. The separately built command-line and MCP tools described in section 7 are not part of the app binary.
  • No developer-operated crash service. If you enable Apple's system diagnostic sharing, Apple may provide anonymised diagnostics under your Apple settings.
  • No advertising. The app shows none.

For App Store purposes, the intended privacy answer is Data Not Collected because the app does not transmit this data to the developer. This does not cover content a user separately and voluntarily submits through GitHub Issues for support, as described in section 9.

4. No connection to your bank

Maturity never requests banking credentials and never connects to a financial institution, uses open banking, scrapes an account or moves money. Every financial record is entered manually. The optional rate service supplies public reference data only; it cannot see bank accounts or transact with them.

5. Network activity

The app can make network connections for two purposes:

  1. Private iCloud sync. Records and synced settings move through Apple's system-managed CloudKit service to your own private container.
  2. Optional published exchange rates. If Show an indicative equivalent is enabled, the app uses api.frankfurter.dev/v1. It requests /currencies, then /latest with base set to the current reporting/base currency code and symbols set to the currency codes present in the tracked position.

Rate requests contain no amounts, balances, institution or account names, record dates, notes, references, record identifiers, API key or app-generated user identifier. Frankfurter and its hosting infrastructure can see the requested currency codes and ordinary network metadata such as the source IP address and standard HTTP/TLS metadata, and may process operational logs under their own practices. The developer does not receive those logs. Leave the setting disabled if you do not want this connection.

Fetched rates are held locally for offline estimates. They are never written into a deposit, ledger entry, balance, interest calculation or yield. The app shows only one secondary ≈ equivalent of the complete tracked position, visibly approximate, dated and identified with its rate source. It appears only when every populated tracked-position currency can be converted; if any required rate is unavailable, no partial numeric equivalent appears. The app does not show separate converted cash or principal totals. Disabling the feature removes the fetched cache.

6. User-triggered exports

Maturity can create a versioned JSON analysis snapshot or a .maturitycsv document package containing separate CSV tables. Exporting is entirely local and occurs only after you open Settings → Data → Export portfolio and press Export. The app does not automatically upload an export, send it to an AI provider or expose its SwiftData store to another process.

Three privacy choices are available:

  • Complete includes institution and account names, references, notes and transaction memos.
  • Financial analysis replaces institution names with aliases and excludes references, notes and memos; account names remain.
  • Redacted, the default, replaces institution and account names with export-specific aliases and excludes all user-entered free text.

Financial analysis and Redacted exports are pseudonymised, not anonymous. Aliases are consistent within one artifact but change when a new export is generated. Stable source UUIDs and relationship identifiers remain, together with amounts, rates, dates, currencies, statuses, calculated views, record timestamps, and app, export and time-zone metadata. Those retained fields can permit linkage or correlation with other information. Review an export before sharing it, particularly when choosing Complete or Financial analysis.

On Mac, the system save panel writes only to a location you select. On iPhone and iPad, the system share sheet controls the destination. Any recipient or destination operates under its own privacy terms. Temporary share files are removed when the flow ends; files left by an interrupted process are removed the next time the Export screen opens.

7. Optional local analysis tools

The Maturity source repository provides separately built command-line and Model Context Protocol (MCP) tools for validating and reading an export. These tools are not part of, launched by or registered by the App Store app. They cannot open Maturity's SwiftData store, private iCloud container or other live app data. They operate only on a JSON export explicitly supplied by the user.

The MCP server is read-only. It can validate an embedded export and filter summary, deposit, scheduled-cashflow, maturity-ladder, liquidity and attention rows already in that artifact. It has no filesystem browsing, export-path access, network transport, telemetry, record mutation or financial-action tools. It cannot create or edit deposits, record transactions, move money or send instructions back to the app. The complete technical boundary is documented on the Docs page.

The local tools do not upload exports by themselves. However, when you supply an export to an MCP client, AI assistant or other analysis application, that client or its service provider receives the artifact and may transmit, process, retain or log it under its own terms. Prefer Redacted exports, inspect the artifact first and use only tools and providers you trust. User-directed sharing does not give Maturity's developer access to the export.

8. Deleting your data

  • Individual records. Delete deposits or transactions inside the app.
  • Delete all data. The in-app action removes synced accounts, deposits, transactions and settings; clears local preferences and the FX cache on that device; and recreates only the empty scaffolding the app needs to operate.
  • Delete the app. This removes the local app container from that device but does not remove data retained in iCloud.

Use the app's Delete all data action when you want to remove the synced portfolio. Deleting the app alone does not remove iCloud data and it may return after a reinstall. The developer has no separate copy of in-app portfolio records to delete; support content you voluntarily submit is handled separately as described below.

9. Support submissions

Support is provided through GitHub Issues. GitHub receives content you voluntarily submit together with your GitHub identity, and issues and comments may be public and processed under GitHub's terms. Do not post financial records, exports, account or certificate references, or other sensitive details. A support submission is an exception to statements that the developer does not receive your data: the developer can read content you choose to post, but does not thereby gain access to your app or iCloud records.

10. Children

Maturity is a personal finance record-keeping tool intended for adults. It is not directed at children and does not collect personal information from them through the app.

11. Your rights

The developer does not receive in-app portfolio records through operation of the app, but may hold support content you voluntarily submit. You control portfolio records through the app and your iCloud settings. Privacy rights concerning data processed by Apple or GitHub are governed by your relationship with those services. Data you separately provide to an MCP client, AI assistant or other recipient is governed by that recipient's terms and controls.

12. Not financial advice

Maturity is a record-keeping and calculation tool. It does not provide financial, investment or taxation advice or recommend products or institutions. Bank statements and quotes remain authoritative.

13. Changes to this policy

Material changes will be published on this page with a revised date and reflected in the App Store privacy information before the corresponding release.

14. Contact

Questions can be raised at github.com/jack-cap/maturity/issues. GitHub receives what you submit and an issue may be public, so do not include financial records or sensitive details. See the support page for product help.
