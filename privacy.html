Privacy policy

Last updated 30 August 2026. Applies to Maturity for iPhone, iPad and Mac.

The short version. Maturity collects nothing. It has no server, no analytics and no account system. Your records stay on your own devices and sync only through your personal iCloud. The developer cannot see your data, and has built no mechanism that would allow it. One optional feature fetches published exchange rates; it sends currency codes and nothing about you, and it is off until you turn it on. Details in section 5.

1. What data the app holds

Maturity stores only what you type into it. That is:

  • Names of banks or institutions, and any account or certificate reference you choose to add
  • Deposit amounts, interest rates, start and maturity dates, interest payment frequency and day-count basis
  • Your cash transaction history, including opening balances, deposits placed, interest received, maturities collected, early breaks, fees and reconciliation adjustments
  • Any notes or memos you write

The app does not ask for your name, email address, date of birth, location, contacts, photos, or any device identifier.

If you enable the optional indicative conversion described in section 5, the app also keeps the exchange rates it fetched and the dates they were published. Those are public reference figures rather than anything about you, they are held only on the device that fetched them, and turning the feature off deletes them.

2. Where that data lives

Records are stored locally on the device, in the app's own storage. If you are signed in to iCloud and have iCloud Drive enabled, they also sync to a private CloudKit container tied to your Apple Account, so your iPhone, iPad and Mac stay in step.

That container is private by design. Apple operates the storage and processes it as part of your iCloud data under Apple's privacy policy. The developer of Maturity has no access to it, no credentials for it, and no ability to read, export or recover its contents.

If you are not signed in to iCloud, the app works normally and keeps everything on the single device.

3. What is not collected

  • No analytics. The app contains no analytics or telemetry SDK, and reports no usage events anywhere.
  • No tracking. No advertising identifiers, no fingerprinting, no cross-app or cross-site tracking, and no data shared with data brokers.
  • No third-party SDKs. The app bundles no third-party code at all. Apple's own frameworks are the only dependencies. The optional rate lookup in section 5 is a plain HTTPS request the app makes itself, not a vendor library, and it has no account or API key.
  • No crash reporting to the developer. If you have opted in to sharing diagnostics with Apple at the system level, Apple may provide the developer with anonymised crash reports. That is an Apple setting you control in Settings, and it contains no data you entered into the app.
  • No advertising. The app shows none.

For App Store purposes, Maturity's privacy label is Data Not Collected.

4. No connection to your bank

Maturity never requests banking credentials and never connects to any financial institution. There is no open banking integration, no screen scraping and no data feed. Every figure in the app is one you entered by hand. This is deliberate: it means there is no credential to be stolen and no third party in the middle.

The optional exchange-rate lookup is not an exception to this. It reads a public list of published reference rates; it has no connection to any bank of yours, no knowledge of your accounts, and no ability to move money.

5. Network activity

The app makes network connections for two purposes, one of which is optional and off by default.

iCloud sync. Your records sync with your own private CloudKit container through Apple's service, as described in section 2. This is the only connection that involves your data at all.

Published exchange rates, if you enable them. If you hold more than one currency you can switch on an indicative conversion, which shows a single estimated total across your currencies. Only then does the app request rates from Frankfurter, an open-source service that republishes reference rates from central banks.

  • What is sent: currency codes, and nothing else. A request looks like ?base=HKD&symbols=AUD,USD. There is no account, no API key, no identifier and no body.
  • What is not sent: no amounts, no balances, no bank names, no dates from your records, no device identifier. Nothing you typed into the app leaves it.
  • What the provider can see: the request itself and, as with any connection to any website, the IP address it came from. That is standard for all web traffic and is not something the app supplies deliberately, but it is the one piece of information that leaves which is not a currency code. If you would rather not have that happen, leave the setting off and the request is never made.
  • What comes back is cached on the device so the figure survives being offline, and is deleted when you turn the setting off.

Rates are never applied to any stored figure. Your balances, interest, yields and per-currency totals are exactly what you recorded; the converted number is shown for scale, marked as an estimate, and dated with the day its rate was published.

6. Deleting your data

  • Individual records. Delete any deposit or transaction inside the app.
  • Local copy. Delete the app from a device to remove its local storage on that device.
  • iCloud copy. On iPhone or iPad, open Settings, tap your name, then iCloud, then Manage Account Storage, and remove Maturity's data. On a Mac, open System Settings, click your name, then iCloud, then Manage. Removing the app alone does not clear the iCloud copy.

Because the developer holds no copy of your data, there is no deletion request to submit and no one to ask. Deleting it on your side deletes it entirely.

7. Children

Maturity is a personal finance tool intended for adults managing their own deposits. It is not directed at children, and it collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

8. Your rights

Privacy law in Australia (the Privacy Act 1988), the European Union (the GDPR), the United Kingdom and elsewhere gives you rights to access, correct, export and erase personal information a company holds about you. Maturity's developer holds no personal information about you, so there is nothing to access or erase on request. You have direct control over every record through the app itself and through your iCloud settings.

9. Not financial advice

Maturity is a record-keeping and calculation tool. It does not provide financial, investment or taxation advice, and it does not recommend products or institutions. Interest and early-break figures it produces are estimates derived from the terms you enter; your bank's statements and quotes are always authoritative. Confirm anything material with your institution or a licensed adviser.

10. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the revised version will be published on this page with a new date at the top. Should a future version of the app ever begin collecting data, that change would be described here and reflected in the App Store privacy label before the release ships.

11. Contact
