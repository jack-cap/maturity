Export your Maturity data

Schema v1 · Local JSON and CSV exports

You stay in control. Maturity creates an export only when you ask. The app hands the finished file to the system save panel or share sheet; it does not upload the file, choose a recipient or send it to the developer.

1. Create an export

In Maturity, open Settings → Data → Export portfolio. Choose a format and privacy mode, review the explanation shown in the app, then press Export.

On Mac, choose a destination in the system save panel. On iPhone and iPad, choose a destination or recipient from the system share sheet. Maturity does not control what happens after you select another app, service or person.

2. Choose a privacy mode

  • Redacted is the default. Institution and account names become export-specific aliases, and user-entered free text is excluded.
  • Financial Analysis aliases institution names and excludes references, notes and memos; account names remain.
  • Complete includes institution and account names, references, notes and transaction memos.

Financial Analysis and Redacted exports are pseudonymised, not anonymous. Every mode retains the amounts, rates, dates, currencies, statuses and relationship identifiers needed to represent the portfolio accurately. Those fields may allow an export to be linked with other information. Review the actual artifact before sharing it and prefer Redacted unless readable labels are necessary.

3. JSON snapshot

The JSON option creates a versioned, machine-readable snapshot. It contains portfolio records together with precomputed summaries, cashflows, maturity periods, liquidity rows, attention items and export provenance. Values remain separated by currency; the export does not invent a combined total for unlike currencies.

Schema information in the file allows compatible software to identify the format. Treat the complete JSON object as sensitive financial information even when Redacted mode is selected.

4. CSV data package

The CSV option creates a .maturitycsv document package containing a manifest.json file and separate, joinable CSV tables. The tables keep related records separate rather than flattening the portfolio into one ambiguous spreadsheet.

On Mac, you can inspect the package in Finder with Show Package Contents. Spreadsheet applications may format dates, identifiers, rates or large numbers in their own way, so compare anything important with Maturity and your institution's records.

5. Share exports safely

Maturity does not transmit an export automatically. If you give one to cloud storage, an AI assistant, analysis software, a financial adviser or another person, that recipient receives the artifact and may transmit, process, retain or log it under its own terms. This user-directed sharing does not give Maturity's developer access to the export.

  • Use Redacted mode unless the recipient genuinely needs readable names or notes.
  • Open and inspect the finished artifact before sharing it.
  • Send it only to recipients and services you trust.
  • Store and delete exported copies using the controls provided by their destination.
  • Do not post an export in a public GitHub issue or other public support channel.

See the privacy policy for the complete description of retained fields, local export handling and third-party destinations.

6. What an export does not do

  • It does not connect to a bank, validate records against a financial institution or move money.
  • It does not upload itself or expose Maturity's live SwiftData store or private iCloud container.
  • It does not make Financial Analysis or Redacted data anonymous.
  • It does not make calculated figures authoritative over bank statements or quotes.

Not financial advice

Maturity is a record-keeping and calculation tool. It does not provide financial, investment or taxation advice and does not recommend products or institutions. Exported figures depend on the records supplied by the user; bank statements and quotes remain authoritative. Confirm anything material with your institution or a licensed adviser.
